01

Title

We record the flow once and ask how far the record can carry the heat on its own.

Good morning, and thank you for coming. My thesis is called Data-based Numerical Simulation of Buoyant Jet Flow. On the right is a small tank. A warm jet comes in from the left, rises, and slowly spreads a warm layer over the cold liquid below. To simulate these ten minutes properly, the computer needs more than nine hours. For the vessel of our laboratory rig, which you will see later, a single reference simulation ran for more than a week. So I asked a simple question. If we record the flow once, how far can that recording carry the heat on its own, especially when the heat itself is what drives the flow?

say your namesmall tank, warm jetnine hours for ten minuteslab vessel: more than a weekrecord once, replay in minutesheat drives the flow

Perkenalan. Tangki kecil, jet hangat membangun lapisan. Simulasi penuh 9 jam lebih untuk 10 menit aliran, dan untuk bejana lab satu simulasi referensi lebih dari seminggu. Ide tesis: rekam alirannya sekali, lalu pindahkan panas dengan rekaman itu saja.

02

Two time scales

The flow settles in seconds, the heat takes minutes.

Let me start with why this matters. In many thermal processes the flow and the heat run on different clocks. Think of a hot-water storage tank, a ladle of molten steel, or a chemical reactor. The flow finds its typical pattern within seconds, while the temperature it carries keeps changing for minutes, sometimes hours. Conventional CFD treats both the same way. It keeps solving the flow at a small time step, long after the flow has settled. For a single design study that's fine. For a digital twin that has to keep up with a running plant, or for an engineer who wants to compare several operating points in an afternoon, it's far too slow.

storage tank, ladle, reactorflow pattern: secondstemperature: minutes to hoursCFD solves the flow every stepdigital twin, design sweep

Banyak proses termal punya dua skala waktu: alirannya stabil dalam hitungan detik, suhunya berkembang dalam menit sampai jam. CFD tetap menghitung aliran di tiap langkah waktu kecil, jadi mahal untuk digital twin atau sapuan banyak titik operasi.

03

The database

Record the motion once, replay the heat many times.

Recurrence CFD makes use of those two clocks. We run one normal simulation in ANSYS Fluent, and while it runs we save six hundred frames, one per second. Each frame records where the content of every cell goes during that second, as cell-to-cell shifts. A replay plays these shifts back in order, adds face swaps between neighbouring cells for the mixing, and keeps the total heat right with a global balance. It moves only the temperature, so the flow equations are never solved again. For the closed tank, the reference run took five hundred and sixty-nine minutes, and one replay takes about two. For the laboratory vessel the gap is wider. Its reference simulation ran for seven and a half days, and a replay takes eight and a half minutes. That is more than a thousand times faster than the reference run. Recording a database is itself a full simulation, nine days for the vessel, so the saving comes when the same database is replayed many times. And since our databases are not recurrent, a replay follows the recorded sequence and does not extrapolate in time. The catch is that the motion inside the database is frozen.

one Fluent run, 600 framescell-to-cell shifts, face swaps, balancetank 569 min vs about 2 minvessel 7.4 days vs 8.5 min, > 1000×solver alone (no images): about 140×recording 9 days, pays back with many replays

Cara kerja rCFD: satu run Fluent disimpan sebagai 600 frame. Replay hanya memindahkan suhu. Tangki: 569 menit lawan sekitar 2 menit. Bejana lab: 7,4 hari lawan 8,5 menit. Perekaman sendiri 9 hari, jadi untung kalau diputar ulang berkali-kali.

04

The open point

Here the temperature moves the flow that carries it.

In this tank, frozen motion is a real problem. Recurrence CFD was built for passive scalars, a dye for example, which the flow carries without ever pushing back. Temperature pushes back. Warm liquid rises and cold liquid sinks, so the temperature shapes the flow that transports it. A replay keeps only half of that relationship. It moves the temperature with the recorded motion, and the temperature has no way to change that motion. Two practical questions come with it. The replay mixes with face swaps, and their strength is a free constant that is set by hand. And a cooled lid acts locally on a temperature field that the database never saw. On the screen are the two versions of the tank I used, with an adiabatic lid on the left and a cooled lid on the right.

passive scalar vs temperaturereplay keeps one directionfree mixing constantcooled lid acts locally

rCFD dibuat untuk skalar pasif. Suhu di sini tidak pasif: suhu menggerakkan aliran. Replay hanya menyimpan satu arah kopling. Dua pertanyaan lain: konstanta pencampuran bebas, dan dinding dingin yang bekerja lokal.

05

Aim and design

How far can one database carry a non-passive scalar?

The aim of the thesis is to find out how far one database can carry a scalar like this. To keep the kinds of error apart, I combined two boundary conditions with two versions of the replay, and every case has its own CFD reference. The tank is small on purpose, seventy-five millimetres square and fifteen deep, with a laminar jet at a Reynolds number of about one hundred, so no turbulence model can blur the comparison. With the adiabatic lid, the database and the reference are the same flow, so any difference comes from the numerics. That gives questions one and two: how the free face-exchange strength trades accuracy against cost, and whether a bounded correction can undo the smearing. The cooled lid brings in the physics. Question three asks how much of the lid cooling the replay reproduces, and question four asks which error is left once the heat loss is right. Question five takes the method out of the tank and into a vessel of our laboratory rig.

CFD reference for every caseadiabatic: numerical error onlycooled lid: adds the physical errorQ1 standard, Q2 extendedQ3, Q4 cooled lidQ5 lab vessel

Tujuan: menguji rCFD pada skalar non-pasif. Desain 2×2: tutup adiabatik dan didinginkan, replay standar dan diperluas. Kasus adiabatik memisahkan galat numerik, tutup dingin menambah galat fisik. Q5 menguji bejana lab.

06

Standard replay

No single face-exchange strength serves both error measures.

I'll start with the standard replay in the adiabatic tank. Its free parameter is the face-exchange strength, f s d. It adds mixing between neighbouring cells, because the frames miss part of the small-scale mixing. I ran seven values, and the two error measures pull in different directions. The mean temperature comes out best with little or no added mixing, at an error of about one kelvin. The height of the warm layer comes out best near zero point five. The reason is the geometry. The outlet sits just below the thermocline, so once the replay smears the thermocline, warm liquid reaches the outlet early and leaves the tank. A numerical error at the thermocline becomes an error in the heat balance, and one constant can't fix both.

one free number, f_sdseven valuesmean T best with little mixinglayer height best near 0.5outlet just below the thermocline

Replay standar punya satu konstanta pencampuran. Suhu rata-rata terbaik tanpa pencampuran tambahan, ketinggian lapisan terbaik di 0,5. Penyebabnya: outlet tepat di bawah front, front yang melebar menguras panas.

07

Extended replay

A bounded anti-diffusive step sharpens the thermocline.

Unphysical diffusion is a known weakness of recurrence CFD, and earlier work adapted the face swaps to the local turbulence. Face swaps can only smooth, though. That pointed me to a different approach, which is to correct the error where it's made, at the thermocline. After each replay step, the extended replay moves a little heat from the cold side to the warm side, up the local gradient. You can think of it as diffusion run backwards, and it sharpens the thermocline. Left alone, a step like that is unstable. In the model problem on the left, the values overshoot beyond the warmest and the coldest liquid in the data. So I limit every exchange, in the spirit of flux-corrected transport, and no cell may leave the range of its neighbours. In the middle, the same front sharpens and stays inside the data range, and on the right its width drops from about nine cells to one. A vertical weight points the exchange across the thermocline, and since every exchange happens between a pair of cells, the total heat is conserved. The extended replay has one more part. Instead of the single constant f s d, it mixes with the diffusivity recorded cell by cell during the reference run.

correct where the error is mademove heat up the gradientunstable without a limitlimiter: stay inside the neighbours' rangevertical weight, heat conserved

Koreksi setelah tiap langkah memindahkan panas melawan gradien sehingga front menajam. Tanpa batas, nilainya keluar rentang data. Dengan limiter gaya FCT, tiap sel tetap dalam rentang tetangganya. Bobot vertikal, panas total tetap.

One record, three fields

Only the extended replay keeps the thermocline as thin as the reference.

Here is what that does in the tank. On the left is the CFD reference, in the middle the standard replay, and on the right the extended replay. The timeline steps through the six instants at which all three fields are stored. Keep an eye on the thin band between warm and cold. By the end of the run, the reference thermocline is about five millimetres thick. The standard replay has spread it to about sixteen, and the extended replay brings it back to about five. You can see the same in the profile on the right, where the extended curve follows the steep reference and the standard curve is clearly flatter. Over the whole run, the error in the mean temperature drops by more than half.

Tiga medan berdampingan. Di akhir run, front referensi sekitar 5 mm, replay standar melebar sampai sekitar 16 mm, extended replay kembali sekitar 5 mm. Galat suhu rata-rata turun lebih dari separuh.

09

Cooled lid

The database must be recorded under cooling.

Now the lid starts losing heat. Before comparing anything here, two conditions have to hold. The replay and the reference must simulate the same flow, and they must be compared at the same instant. Heat can only leave through the lid as fast as the flow brings warm liquid up to it. The liquid under the lid cools, gets heavier and sinks, and warmer liquid moves in to take its place. A database recorded with an adiabatic lid has never seen that motion. Replayed from it, the tank loses only sixty-three per cent of the heat that the reference loses through the lid, and neither more mixing nor the correction gets it back. Once I record the database with the cooling switched on, the standard replay already reaches ninety-eight per cent. A boundary condition can switch on a new flow regime, and the database has to contain it. The film behind this button shows the cooled-lid reference over the whole run, if you'd like to see that motion.

heat leaves as fast as motion brings warm watercooled water sinksadiabatic database: 63 %mixing or correction do not helpcooled database: 98 %

Panas keluar lewat tutup hanya secepat gerakan membawa air hangat ke atas. Rekaman adiabatik tidak memuat gerakan itu: hanya 63 %. Dengan basis data yang direkam saat pendinginan: 98 %.

10

Correction under cooling

The correction recovers both the heat content and its vertical distribution.

With the right database, the amount of heat is close, but it sits in the wrong place. At the end of the run, the profile of the standard replay is two point eight kelvin away from the reference, as a root mean square. The top layer is too cold and the bulk underneath is too warm. With the correction at its working point, s equal to zero point five five, that error falls to zero point four one kelvin. The final mean temperature lands within zero point zero six kelvin of the reference, on a total cooling of about nine and a half kelvin. Through the lid, the corrected replay removes a little more heat than the reference, about one hundred and two per cent. I picked the working point from the end profile and the end temperature.

right amount, wrong placestandard profile 2.8 Ktop too cold, bulk too warms = 0.55: profile 0.41 Kend within 0.06 K, lid 102 %

Dengan basis data yang benar, jumlah panas hampir benar tetapi letaknya salah (profil 2,8 K). Dengan koreksi s = 0,55: profil 0,41 K, suhu akhir dalam 0,06 K dari referensi. Panas lewat tutup 102 %.

11

What stays open

During filling, the replayed layers separate too slowly.

One error survives everything local I tried. While the warm liquid is still flowing in, the reference separates the layers quickly, and the replay does it too slowly. Measured as the heterogeneity of the field, the gap is two point two five kelvin during the warm inflow. So I tried something else. I took the vertical convective heat flux from the reference run and added it to the replay. The error dropped to zero point four five kelvin without any fitted number, which tells me that the missing piece is transport. I keep the flux as a diagnostic test and leave it out of the method, because the tank then ends zero point eight six kelvin too cold, and the flux is valid only for the run it came from.

one error survives2.25 K during warm inflowno local rule moves itrecorded vertical flux: 0.45 Ka test, ends 0.86 K too cold

Satu galat bertahan: lapisan memisah terlalu lambat saat tangki terisi (2,25 K). Fluks vertikal yang direkam menurunkannya ke 0,45 K tanpa angka fit, jadi yang hilang adalah transport. Tapi itu uji, bukan komponen: tangki berakhir 0,86 K terlalu dingin.

12

Laboratory vessel

The operators transfer to the laboratory vessel, but no replay can add the motion missing from the database.

For the last question I left the closed tank. Our department has a laboratory rig with a through-flow vessel of seven and a half litres. A warm pulse comes in either through the lid line or through the floor line and leaves through a drain at half height, and seven thermocouples follow the temperature. I recorded one database with the lid feed and replayed both cases from it. Against the reference simulation of each case, the extended replay lowers the mean error over the seven heights from one point two eight to zero point seven seven kelvin with the lid feed, and from one point six one to zero point six eight with the floor feed. Look at the floor feed in the reference, though. The warm water climbs the wall in plumes. The lid-fed database never contained that motion, so no replay can produce it. The extended replay keeps sharp structures, only in other places. What carried over is the set of operators, and each case needed its own setting. Finally, I held the replays to the thermocouples by nudging. With the floor feed, the extended replay then stays closer to the rig, zero point five one against zero point eight one kelvin, although a nudged run uses the measurement and is no prediction.

7.5-litre through-flow vessellid feed or floor feed, one lid-fed database1.28 to 0.77 K, 1.61 to 0.68 Kfloor plumes along the wallreplay cannot create them

Bejana rig 7,5 liter, diumpan dari tutup atau dasar, keduanya di-replay dari satu basis data umpan tutup. Extended replay menurunkan galat (1,28 ke 0,77 K dan 1,61 ke 0,68 K), tetapi plume dasar tidak bisa diciptakan karena tidak ada di rekaman.

13

Answers

A non-passive scalar can be replayed if the correction acts at the thermocline and the database contains the flow regime.

So, back to the five questions. For the first, there is no single best face-exchange strength. Heat content and stratification want different values, because an outlet near the thermocline turns a numerical error into an energy error. For the second, the answer is yes, at least in the closed tank. The bounded correction restores the thermocline without leaving the range of the data, and its direction matters more than its strength. For the third, the replay loses the right amount of heat only from a database recorded under cooling, and with the correction the heat also ends up in the right place. For the fourth, what remains is the slow separation of the layers while the tank fills, and that is missing transport. And for the fifth, the operators work in the open vessel too, but they can't add motion that the database doesn't hold. All of this rests on one tank, one mesh and one vessel, compared with simulated references.

Q1 no single best strengthQ2 yes, in the closed tankQ3 database recorded under coolingQ4 missing transportQ5 operators transfer, motion does notone tank, one mesh, one vessel

Jawaban Q1–Q5: tak ada satu kekuatan pencampuran terbaik; koreksi terbatas membangun ulang front; perlu rekaman dengan pendinginan; sisa galat = transport yang hilang; operator bisa dibawa ke bejana, gerakan yang tak terekam tidak.

14

Where learning fits

The two remaining errors are candidates for data-driven models.

That leaves two open problems. The correction strength is still found by sweeping values for each case, and the replay can't invent motion that the database lacks. A recurrence database is a good place to start learning, since it holds many states of one flow and a trained model is cheap to run. The first step I'd take is a small network that predicts the local correction strength from the replayed field. It would work inside the limiter, so the bounds and the conservation stay in place. Further out, a neural operator could learn the step from one temperature field to the next, and a sequence model, a transformer or an xLSTM for example, could choose which frame or which database to play next. I haven't tried any of these in the thesis. They also matter for a digital twin. The replay is already fast, and the vessel runs showed that it can be held to sensor data. That matters, because a replay running forward on its own tends to collect error over time. What it still needs is a database for every regime the plant can enter, and a correction strength that doesn't depend on a reference.

strength chosen by sweepsmissing motionlearned strength inside the limiterneural operator, sequence modelnot tried heredigital twin: fast, nudged, needs regimes

Dua masalah tersisa: kekuatan koreksi masih dipilih dengan sapuan, dan gerakan yang tidak ada di rekaman. Langkah pertama: jaringan kecil memprediksi kekuatan lokal di dalam limiter. Lalu neural operator dan model urutan. Belum dicoba di tesis. Untuk digital twin: replay sudah cepat dan bisa diselaraskan dengan sensor, tetapi butuh basis data untuk tiap rezim dan kekuatan koreksi yang tidak bergantung referensi.
